\begin{abstract}
% ==============================================================
Analytic continual learning (ACL) methods maintain a recursive least-squares
(RLS) head over frozen foundation features, relying on a forgetting factor
$f=1$ (\emph{absolute memory}) to emulate a joint solution incrementally.
We study when this default fails for continual \emph{visual regression} where
domain label scales and feature-to-target drift may co-exist.
We decompose forgetting benefit into two mechanisms---
\emph{magnitude mismatch} (inter-domain label scale differences, removed by
per-domain mean normalization) and \emph{residual negative transfer}
(forgetting benefit persisting after normalization; may reflect mapping drift,
noise heteroscedasticity, or ridge-bias interactions).  Under explicit homoskedastic,
equal-information conditions we establish the boundary at which $f^*=1$, and
give an exact finite-horizon directional condition for beneficial forgetting
under an isotropic model.  We then propose an exemplar-free, test-set-free
selector that chooses one factor per domain boundary from held-out
quadratic sufficient statistics.  Both fitting and balanced
validation histories are accumulated, so total selector memory is $O(d^2)$
and independent of the number of domains.  A directly observable validation-gain score
measures the relative validation-risk reduction over $f=1$.  Our evaluation
protocol includes crowd counting, age estimation, depth, aesthetics and image
quality, explicit VFF-RLS and matched-feature baselines, negative cases, and
content-disjoint splitting.  The release protocol rejects results without
machine-readable provenance, exact data-manifest hashes, and a clean code
commit.
\end{abstract}

% ==============================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{Frozen features + analytic heads: the no-replay paradigm.}
Large pretrained vision models (DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2},
CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning}) extract rich, transferable features
without fine-tuning. Pairing a \emph{frozen} backbone with a closed-form
regression head enables exemplar-free continual learning with reduced raw-data
retention, lightweight updates (no backpropagation), and
analytically exact. Recent analytic continual learning (ACL) works---
ACIL~\cite{zhuang2022acil}, RanPAC~\cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac},
DS-AL~\cite{yang2024dsal}, GACL~\cite{gao2024gacl}---exploit recursive
least squares (RLS) to show that sequential single-pass updates match joint
training exactly under $f=1$.

\paragraph{The overlooked regression setting.}
Virtually all ACL work targets \emph{classification}, where $f=1$ (absolute
memory) is celebrated as making incremental equivalent to joint.
But visual \emph{regression}---crowd counting, age estimation, depth
prediction---is commonplace in data-retention-constrained, resource-limited
deployments, and it introduces a dimension absent from classification:
\emph{continuous, drifting label scales across domains}.
We ask: \textbf{is $f=1$ still optimal when domains exhibit heterogeneous
label distributions or residual negative transfer in feature-to-target mappings?}

\paragraph{Motivating observation.}
Exploratory crowd-counting runs suggested that the preferred factor may
depend on domain order even after target normalization.  Those runs used
inconsistent dataset roots and are not evidence.  They motivate the
controlled, identical-manifest order study specified in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\paragraph{Our contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Mechanism-decomposition protocol.} We use per-domain mean
    normalization to test whether a forgetting benefit is explained by
    first-order target-scale mismatch or persists as residual negative
    transfer.
  \item \textbf{Theory and decision score.} Under an isotropic approximation
    we state the additional conditions required for $f^*=1$, derive an exact
    finite-horizon directional condition for $f^*<1$, and operationalize a
    verifiable train-only validation-gain score computable from sufficient
    statistics.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive selector.} A per-boundary selector using
    held-out sufficient statistics---no test data, no exemplars,
    global candidate-grid search and $O(d^2)$ total storage.
  \item \textbf{Auditable evaluation.} We compare with a domain-batched
    adaptation of robust VFF-RLS~\cite{paleologu2008vff}, matched image-only
    SGD/RLS, RanPAC-style random features, and deliberate negative examples.
    Every result carries its configuration hash, code commit and runtime
    arguments; label-defined or non-semantic proxy domains are not presented
    as natural continual domains.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{Analytic continual learning.}
ACIL~\cite{zhuang2022acil} established that recursive feature
accumulation with $f=1$ makes class-incremental learning equivalent to
joint offline training. RanPAC~\cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac} extends this
with random projections for kernel approximation.
DS-AL and GACL~\cite{yang2024dsal,gao2024gacl} scale to larger class
sets. These methods target classification and use absolute memorization.
They motivate our matched $f=1$ reference, but do not establish that the
same weighting is optimal for heterogeneous visual regression.

\paragraph{Lifelong crowd counting.}
FLCB~\cite{flcb2022} introduced a domain-incremental self-distillation
benchmark for lifelong crowd counting.  It uses end-to-end density
regression with old-model constraints rather than a frozen representation
and closed-form head.
Our setting differs fundamentally: we freeze the backbone and use a
closed-form analytic head, making our update one pass per domain with no
backpropagation. This places us in the analytic CL tradition rather than
the deep distillation tradition.

\paragraph{Continual blind IQA.}
Several works study continual learning for blind image quality assessment
(BIQA). BIQA-CL~\cite{zhang2023biqa_cl} appends one prediction head per
dataset, regularizes the heads during sequential backpropagation, and uses an
adaptive weighted sum at inference. LIQA~\cite{liqa2022} instead generates
pseudo-features for old distortions and applies split-and-merge distillation;
its final regressor is a single-head, task-agnostic model. TSN-IQA
~\cite{chen2024tsn_iqa} freezes convolution filters but learns task-specific
normalization parameters and prediction heads, then combines their outputs
with a lightweight K-means gating mechanism. SILF~\cite{ma2023silf} uses
dynamic parameter isolation, cross-task reuse, and selective pruning under a
memory budget. These are essential task-specific comparators, not variants of
one common multi-head design.  Our method differs in using a single shared
closed-form ridge head without backpropagation or pseudo-feature replay, while
remaining task-aware because the domain-specific target scale $s_k$ is needed
at inference.

\paragraph{Adaptive forgetting in RLS and analytic output layers.}
Variable forgetting factor RLS (VFF-RLS)~\cite{leung1993vff} and
directional forgetting~\cite{kulhavy1987directional} adapt $f$ to
track non-stationary signals in control and signal processing.
Adaptive-forgetting OS-ELM~\cite{lim2013affoselm} and DOS-ELM
~\cite{cao2019doselm} are especially close mathematically: they combine
fixed/random features, an RLS-type analytic output layer, and a dynamically
adjusted forgetting factor, including regression experiments.  A concept
drift map has also been used to regulate OS-ELM forgetting
~\cite{yu2019driftmap}.  Therefore, our contribution is not the invention of
adaptive forgetting for a closed-form regression head.  It is the
domain-balanced, train-only sufficient-statistic selection rule and its study
with frozen visual foundation features under task-aware domain-incremental
regression.  SIFt-RLS~\cite{lai2024sift} further shows that scalar exponential
forgetting can be too coarse by forgetting only in the incoming information
subspace; it is a required directional baseline for the final evaluation.
A fixed-$f$ oracle is \emph{not} a VFF-RLS method.  We therefore include a
domain-batched implementation of the robust error/noise-power rule of
Paleologu et al.~\cite{paleologu2008vff}.  Its original rule is sample-wise;
our adaptation estimates the same powers on held-out samples from the
incoming known domain and applies one factor at that boundary.  We report
this adaptation explicitly rather than presenting it as an exact reproduction
of the original streaming system-identification protocol.

\paragraph{Continual regression.}
CLeaR~\cite{he2021clear} provides an adaptive buffered framework for
non-stationary regression, while Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2024statistical}
analyze regularization-based continual learning on sequences of linear
regression tasks. EWC~\cite{kirkpatrick2017ewc} and LwF~\cite{li2017lwf}
can also be adapted to regression; they require backpropagation and,
respectively, Fisher-state or old-model predictions, but not necessarily
stored exemplars. Our setting is narrower: frozen visual features, a shared
closed-form head, known domain boundaries, and no raw-example replay.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Problem Setting}

\paragraph{Task-aware domain-incremental regression.}
We adopt the \emph{task-aware domain-incremental} (TADIL) setting: the
domain identity $k$ is known during both training and inference, but
original data from previous domains is unavailable after processing.
This models deployments with restricted raw-data retention.  It does not,
by itself, provide a formal privacy guarantee: sufficient statistics may
still leak information without an explicit privacy mechanism.
We make no assumption of a shared feature-to-target mapping across domains;
each domain may have its own optimal regressor $w_k$.

Formally, we observe a sequence of $T$ domains $k=1,\dots,T$.
Domain $k$ provides $n_k$ samples $\{(x_i, y_i)\}$ where
$x_i \in \R^d$ are features extracted by a \emph{frozen} vision
backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B/14, $d=768$) and $y_i$ is either an image-level
scalar target (age, aesthetics, IQA, total count) or a patch-level scalar
target (crowd patch count or patch depth).
We assume a linear model per domain: $y = x^\top w_k + \varepsilon_k$
where $\varepsilon_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_k^2)$.
After processing domain $k$, the original data is discarded---no exemplar
replay, no access to previous raw data.

\subsection{Recursive Ridge Regression with Forgetting}

The closed-form continual update accumulates second-order sufficient
statistics with a per-step forgetting factor $f_k \in (0,1]$:
\begin{align}
  R_k &= f_k R_{k-1} + X_k^\top X_k, \label{eq:R}\\
  C_k &= f_k C_{k-1} + X_k^\top y_k, \label{eq:C}\\
  W_k &= (R_k + \lambda I)^{-1} C_k. \label{eq:W}
\end{align}
Setting $f_k=1$ for all $k$ recovers the joint ridge solution
(Proposition~\ref{prop:fstar1}).
Setting $f_k<1$ exponentially down-weights older domains.

\paragraph{Crowd dual-head prediction.}
Each $518{\times}518$ image yields $37{\times}37=1369$ DINOv2 patch
tokens.  A patch ridge head regresses a DCT-5-smoothed patch-count target,
while a second image head regresses total count from the mean-pooled token.
The final count is
$0.25\sum_p\hat y_p+0.75\hat y_{\mathrm{image}}$.
Age, aesthetics, and IQA use one mean-pooled image head.  Depth uses a
patch head whose targets are mean metric depths within the corresponding
patches. A ridge parameter $\lambda=100$ is used throughout.

\paragraph{Memory and computational complexity.}
Each deployed ridge head stores one augmented $d{\times}d$ matrix $R$ and
one $d{\times}1$ vector $C$ (crowd counting deploys two such heads).  The
selector stores one accumulated fitting
quadratic and one accumulated balanced-validation quadratic, rather than a
matrix for every historical domain.  Its total state is therefore $O(d^2)$,
independent of $T$.  With float64 and an augmented dimension of 769, each
quadratic matrix occupies approximately 4.73\,MB.
Per-domain update cost is $O(n_k d^2)$ for accumulation and $O(d^3)$ for
solving; selecting from $B$ globally enumerated factors requires $B$ linear
solves and adds $O(Bd^3)$ at a boundary ($B$ is fixed and independent of $T$).
Critically, \emph{no GPU is required} for the RLS update or $f$ selection;
only the DINOv2 forward pass benefits from GPU acceleration.

\subsection{Magnitude Normalization}
\label{sec:norm}

Crowd counts vary substantially across datasets (SHB: mean~123,
JHU: mean~372, SHA: mean~541), a ${\sim}4\times$ spread.
When this scale mismatch dominates, a forgetting factor $f<1$ merely
compensates for magnitude differences rather than residual negative transfer.
To isolate the two mechanisms, we optionally normalize targets per domain
by the domain training mean before accumulation:
$\tilde{y}_{ki} = y_{ki} / \mu_k$, where $\mu_k =
\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_i y_{ki}$.
This normalization is applied consistently to both $R$ and $C$ updates
and inverted at prediction time.

\subsection{Adaptive Forgetting Selector}
\label{sec:adaptive}

At each domain boundary, we select $f_k$ using a held-out validation procedure
over within-domain
sufficient statistics:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Split:} Divide domain $k$'s training data into deterministic
    80/20 fit and held-out partitions. For KADID, complete reference-image
    groups rather than individual distortions are held out.
  \item \textbf{Fit sufficient statistics:}
    $R^{\mathrm{fit}}_k = X_{\mathrm{fit}}^\top X_{\mathrm{fit}}$,
    $C^{\mathrm{fit}}_k = X_{\mathrm{fit}}^\top y_{\mathrm{fit}}$.
  \item \textbf{Candidate objective:} For $f$ in a predeclared grid
    $\mathcal F\subset[f_{\min},1]$, form
    $R(f) = f\,\bar R^{\mathrm{fit}}_{k-1} + R^{\mathrm{fit}}_k$,
    $C(f) = f\,\bar C^{\mathrm{fit}}_{k-1} + C^{\mathrm{fit}}_k$,
    $W(f) = (R(f)+\lambda I)^{-1} C(f)$.
  \item \textbf{Balanced validation:} Domain $j$ contributes the normalized
    validation MSE
    $[W^\top R_j^vW-2W^\top C_j^v+S_j^v]/n_j^v$.
    Its average needs only the running sums
    $\sum_jR_j^v/n_j^v$, $\sum_jC_j^v/n_j^v$ and
    $\sum_jS_j^v/n_j^v$.  We enumerate the complete grid and retain the
    global grid minimizer. A continuous refinement is only an ablation because
    $\hat J_k(f)$ has not been proven unimodal.
  \item \textbf{Deploy and update state:} Update the deployed head using
    \emph{all} of domain $k$'s data and the selected $f_k^*$. Separately,
    update the selector's fitting state as
    $\bar R_k^{\mathrm{fit}}=f_k^*\bar R_{k-1}^{\mathrm{fit}}+
    R_k^{\mathrm{fit}}$; held-out samples remain only in the balanced
    validation state.
\end{enumerate}

The observable validation-gain score is the relative validation-risk reduction
\begin{equation}
  G_k =
  \frac{\hat J_k(1)-\min_{f\in\mathcal F}\hat J_k(f)}
       {\max(|\hat J_k(1)|,\epsilon)}.
  \label{eq:validation_gain}
\end{equation}
When $G_k$ is below a predeclared numerical tolerance, the selector
abstains and uses $f_k=1$.  This score and $f_k^*$ are computed from the same
train-only objective. It measures actionable validation improvement, not a
physically identified concept-drift magnitude.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Setup and Notation}

Under an isotropic approximation $\E[xx^\top] = s_k I$, define domain
information $\mu_k := n_k s_k$ and age $\tau_k := T-k$.  For analysis,
first consider a common factor $f$ across a finite horizon.  Then
\begin{equation}
  W(f)=\frac{\sum_k f^{\tau_k}\mu_kw_k+\sum_kf^{\tau_k}\nu_k}
             {D(f)},\qquad
  D(f):=\sum_kf^{\tau_k}\mu_k+\lambda .
\end{equation}
Writing $\bar W(f)=\E[W(f)]$ and
$V(f)=\sum_kf^{2\tau_k}\mu_kr_k/D(f)^2$, the balanced excess risk is
\begin{equation}
  J(f)=\frac1T\sum_k\rho_k\norm{\bar W(f)-w_k}^2
       +d\bar\rho V(f).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[A precise no-drift boundary]
\label{prop:fstar1}
Assume (i) $w_k=w$ for every domain, (ii) $\mu_k=\mu$,
(iii) $r_k=r$, and (iv) $\lambda=0$.  Then $f=1$ minimizes expected
prediction risk.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The estimator is unbiased.  With $a_k=f^{\tau_k}$, its variance is
proportional to $\sum_ka_k^2/(\sum_ka_k)^2$.  Cauchy--Schwarz lower-bounds
this quantity by $1/T$, with equality exactly when all $a_k$ are equal.
For $f\in(0,1]$, equality is attained at $f=1$. \qed
\end{proof}

No mapping drift alone is therefore not sufficient outside these conditions.
Unequal noise, unequal information, or non-negligible ridge bias can make
$f<1$ beneficial even when all $w_k$ coincide; such a gain must not be labeled
as concept-drift evidence.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-horizon directional condition]
\label{prop:fstar_lt1}
At $f=1$, define $D=M+\lambda$, $N=\sum_k\mu_kw_k$,
$Q=\sum_k\mu_kr_k$, and
$D'=\sum_k\tau_k\mu_k$, $N'=\sum_k\tau_k\mu_kw_k$,
$Q'=\sum_k2\tau_k\mu_kr_k$.  Let
\begin{align}
 \bar W' &= (N'D-ND')/D^2,\\
 V' &= Q'/D^2-2QD'/D^3.
\end{align}
If
\begin{equation}
 J'_-(1)=\frac2T\sum_k\rho_k(\bar W(1)-w_k)^\top\bar W'
          +d\bar\rho V' >0,
 \label{eq:suff_cond}
\end{equation}
then every minimizer over $(0,1]$ satisfies $f^*<1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the finite sums defining $\bar W(f)=N(f)/D(f)$ and
$V(f)=Q(f)/D(f)^2$.  If the left derivative at one is positive, moving to a
sufficiently nearby smaller factor strictly reduces $J$, so one cannot be a
minimizer. \qed
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:suff_cond} retains the exact finite-horizon variance
derivative.  The implemented validation-gain score $G_k$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:validation_gain} is an empirical decision statistic for the
actual per-boundary objective; it is not an exact estimator of the derivative
or of $f^*$.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Datasets and Evaluation}

\paragraph{Crowd counting.}
We use ShanghaiTech Part A (SHA; 300/182 train/test, mean count 541) and
Part B (SHB; 400/316, mean count 123)~\cite{zhang2016shanghaitech},
JHU-Crowd++ (JHU; 2272/500 train/val, mean count 372)
~\cite{sindagi2019jhu}, and UCF-QNRF (QNRF; 1201/334, mean count
815)~\cite{idrees2018qnrf}.
Evaluation: relative MAE ($\relmae = \mathrm{MAE}/\mu_{\mathrm{test}}$)
computed per domain; final score is the average across all domains in
the sequence (\emph{balanced multi-domain average}).
We also report each dataset's raw-count MAE and RMSE, plus nBwT
(negative backward transfer; higher = less forgetting of old domains).

\paragraph{Age estimation.}
We use UTKFace~\cite{zhifei2017utkface} (23k images, ages 1--116), with the diagnostic
label-defined split UTK-Young (ages 1--25) and UTK-Old (45--90), and
AgeDB~\cite{moschoglou2017agedb} (12k images, faces in the wild) for
cross-dataset sequences.
The label-defined split is not treated as a natural continual domain because
knowing its identity reveals the target range. We report MAE in years and
RMSE in addition to balanced $\relmae$.

\paragraph{Extended regression tasks.}
We additionally evaluate on three benchmarks spanning diverse visual
regression modalities.
\emph{KADID-10k}~\cite{kadid10k} contains 10{,}125 distorted images
generated from 81 reference images, 25 distortion types, and 5 severity
levels. We partition the distortion types into 5 distortion-family
domains (Noise, Blur, Color, Compression, Spatial), each with 500
training and 425 test images.
\emph{AVA Aesthetics}~\cite{ava} is a 10\% subset (${\approx}25$k
images) of the full AVA dataset; we create 3 score-tier domains by
splitting the mean aesthetic score distribution at its 33rd and 67th
percentiles (AVA-Low, AVA-Mid, AVA-High), each with 500 train and
${\approx}1700$ test images.
\emph{NYU Depth V2}~\cite{nyuv2} is converted into three source-batch
proxy domains derived from the available train/validation/test source
partitions; no scene metadata is available in this conversion.  We regress
mean metric depth for each DINOv2 patch, not one mean depth per image.
This experiment is a pipeline/negative-control check, not evidence from
natural semantic depth domains.
All three use the same frozen DINOv2 backbone and ridge head.

\paragraph{Backbone and head.}
All experiments use a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 backbone.
Features are $d=768$-dimensional patch tokens; image-level tasks mean-pool
them, whereas crowd patch counting and depth preserve the $37{\times}37$
patch grid.
Ridge regularization $\lambda=100$.
For crowd counting we use the dual-head prediction described in
Section~\ref{sec:method}.  For IQA/AVA we report SRCC, PLCC, MAE, and
RMSE; for depth we report AbsRel, RMSE, $\delta_1/\delta_2/\delta_3$,
and SILog.

\subsection{Theory--Selector Consistency}

\paragraph{What is directly verifiable.}
The implemented score is $G_k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:validation_gain}: the
relative reduction of a balanced held-out quadratic objective. Each result
artifact stores $G_k$, $\hat J_k(1)$, the selected objective, and the complete
factor choice at every boundary. This makes the selector's decision auditable
without test labels. We deliberately do not rename $G_k$ a ``concept-drift
magnitude,'' because label noise, unequal information, ridge bias, and
representation error can also change it.

\paragraph{Scope of the propositions.}
Propositions~\ref{prop:fstar1}--\ref{prop:fstar_lt1} analyze a common fixed
factor under an isotropic finite-horizon model. The deployed algorithm chooses
a causal sequence $(f_2,\ldots,f_T)$, under which domain $j$ receives weight
$\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{T}f_\ell$. The propositions explain why $f=1$ needs
stronger conditions than ``no mapping drift'' and provide a local directional
diagnostic, but they are not a regret or optimality guarantee for the greedy
selector. A clean theory artifact comparing train-only predictions with
fixed-factor test oracles is required before any numerical accuracy claim is
restored.

\paragraph{Why a local pair score is insufficient.}
The candidate model at boundary $k$ depends on the entire accumulated
sufficient-statistic state, so two identical adjacent-domain pairs can imply
different optimal discounts when their earlier histories differ. This
structural observation motivates evaluating candidate factors against the
balanced history rather than mapping a pairwise feature distance directly to
$f_k$. The earlier numerical pairwise study remains provisional pending the
same data-identity and provenance checks as the main results.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{Isotropic approximation.}
Propositions~1--2 rely on $\Sigma_k = s_k I$; real DINOv2 features are
non-isotropic, and the common-$f$ analysis does not guarantee the causal
per-boundary selector.  An anisotropic analysis and a selector stability or
regret bound remain open.

\paragraph{Natural domains and replication.}
AVA score tiers and UTK age bands are label-defined; NYU source batches are
proxy domains. They cannot establish natural-domain generalization. The final
claim requires stable positive results on at least two independent,
non-label-defined domain sequences with repeated seeds and confidence
intervals.

\paragraph{Linear head capacity and directional forgetting.}
Our experiments use scalar exponential forgetting in a linear ridge head.
Random-feature expansion changes both capacity and storage and does not
replace a matched directional-forgetting comparison. DOS-ELM and SIFt-RLS
remain mandatory baselines for deciding whether the proposed selector is
better than prior analytic adaptation.

\paragraph{Greedy per-boundary selector.}
The adaptive selector makes a locally optimal choice at each domain
boundary using the balanced validation loss up to that point.
An earlier KADID-10k snapshot suggested that this greedy approach can fail
when the globally optimal factor requires coordinated discounting, but that
snapshot is invalid under the corrected reference-group validation protocol
and must be rerun.
A non-greedy variant---e.g., jointly optimizing $f$ across all
boundaries or using a lookahead horizon---could address this, at the
cost of $O(|\mathcal{F}|^T)$ complexity.

\paragraph{Deep distillation baselines.}
We do not fully implement FLCB's deep distillation variant, which
operates in a different computational budget (end-to-end training,
old-model constraints). Comparison is therefore at the ``closed-form
analytic CL'' level, not against deep lifelong methods.

\paragraph{Metric alignment and uncertainty.}
The selector optimizes normalized MSE, whereas some headline summaries use
relative MAE. Final evaluation must report their empirical alignment, task
standard metrics, multiple fixed manifests/seeds, and paired confidence
intervals. Small gains without these checks are not treated as reliable.

% ==============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ==============================================================

This work asks a narrower question than prior drafts: in task-aware
domain-incremental visual regression with frozen foundation features and a
shared ridge head, can train-only balanced sufficient statistics decide when
historical information should be discounted?  The method separates
first-order target-scale mismatch from residual negative transfer, enumerates
a global factor grid at each boundary, and abstains when the held-out gain
$G_k$ is negligible.  Its selector state is $O(d^2)$ and requires neither old
raw examples nor test labels.

The current manuscript does not claim ``first,'' SOTA, or free gains.
Adaptive-forgetting OS-ELM and directional RLS are clear prior art, the theory
covers a simplified common-factor model, and several earlier numerical
artifacts are invalidated by stricter data-identity or grouped-validation
checks.  The paper-level conclusion will be restored only after clean reruns
show stable gains on at least two independent natural-domain sequences,
compare against DOS-ELM and SIFt-RLS, report task-standard metrics with
uncertainty, and pass the release validator.  Until then, RRCL is a promising,
falsifiable method proposal rather than a completed SOTA result.
