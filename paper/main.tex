\begin{abstract}
% ==============================================================
Analytic continual learning can update a recursive least-squares (RLS) head
over frozen features without replay.  With forgetting factor $f=1$, its
sufficient statistics exactly recover the matched joint ridge solution, but
this equivalence does not imply that uniform historical weighting minimizes
balanced risk across heterogeneous domains. We separate whether a frozen
scalar-factor family contains a useful weighting from whether an allowed
train-only rule selects it. Under common-mapping, equal-information,
equal-noise and zero-ridge-bias
conditions, $f=1$ minimizes expected risk; an isotropic finite-horizon analysis
gives a local condition for beneficial forgetting. A scoped two-world example
also shows that unrestricted train--test covariance shift can make the
test-optimal factor non-identifiable from complete training information.

We retain four preregistered or frozen tests. In a $2^4$ controlled study, all
16 cells remain below the prespecified $0.5\%$ material scalar-grid
opportunity threshold. On a previously unseen FDST source, the diagnostic
Oracle again selects $f=1$ within the frozen scalar grid; this is an external
class-conditional boundary, not confirmation of the development gap. We then
broaden the synthetic task distribution and
train a KNN curve selector on 512 simulator tasks, tune it on 128, and evaluate
once on 256 held-out tasks. Forty test tasks have at least $0.5\%$ scalar-grid
Oracle opportunity. On this subset the learned selector improves over $f=1$ by
$0.520\%$ (95\% interval $[0.330\%,0.728\%]$), but its regret is higher than
the frozen analytic rule's ($0.701\%$ versus $0.429\%$), so the preregistered
four-part composite success criterion is not met. The learned rule nevertheless
has positive all-task utility and lower mean-domain harm, whereas the analytic
rule has lower opportunity-subset regret; neither dominates. Matched SIFt-RLS
and DOS-ELM-style baselines yield only $0.109\%$ and $0.118\%$ mean utility on
the same subset. These are 8-dimensional synthetic linear-head comparisons,
not a fair natural-feature test of directional forgetting.

Finally, two low-resolution crowd-counting development diagnostics retain
rule failures: a
reliability proxy fails its JHU/QNRF material-decision gate, while an isotropic
factor predictor has worse factor MAE than constant $f=1$ and worsens matched
test MSE by $2.876\%$ despite a $2.579\%$ scalar-grid Oracle opportunity.
These results bound the studied scalar rules, finite grids and task
distributions; they neither establish a validated adaptive algorithm nor a
universal train-only impossibility or absence of directional-weighting
opportunity.
Manifests, locks, hashes and independent recomputation cover every retained
claim set.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{Joint equivalence is not cross-domain optimality.}
Large pretrained vision models such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2023dinov2} and
CLIP~\citep{radford2021learning} provide transferable frozen features.  A
closed-form ridge head can then be updated without backpropagation or raw-
example replay.  Analytic continual-learning methods such as
ACIL~\citep{zhuang2022acil}, RanPAC~\citep{mcdonnell2023ranpac},
DS-AL~\citep{yang2024dsal} and GACL~\citep{gao2024gacl} exploit the fact that
accumulating all sufficient statistics with $f=1$ is algebraically equivalent
to fitting the matched joint head.  This identity specifies a strong and
reproducible reference; it is not a task-performance upper bound.  When
domains differ in information, noise, label scale or feature-to-target
mapping, a balanced multi-domain objective need not prefer the same weights
as pooled empirical risk.

\paragraph{The opportunity--selection question.}
Visual regression makes this distinction explicit because errors are
continuous and domain scales can differ substantially.  A test sweep may show
that down-weighting some historical domains could improve balanced risk, but
such an oracle does not define a deployable rule.  A causal learner must decide
from its permitted training-side state, without consuming test labels or
retroactively changing the rule.  We therefore ask two linked but distinct
questions: \textbf{when does a predeclared scalar-factor family contain useful
non-uniform historical weighting, and can a prespecified train-only statistic
select it with positive decision utility?}

\paragraph{A falsification rather than an algorithm-success study.}
We answer these questions in task-aware analytic continual visual regression
with a frozen DINOv2 representation and shared ridge head.  Our development
protocol retains two negative outcomes: a reliability-reweighting rule fails
its material-decision gate, and an isotropic fixed-factor predictor is
moderately correlated with a test oracle but performs worse than the constant
$f=1$ rule when deployed. This pattern motivates a class-conditional
distinction between weighting headroom and selection from a restricted
information set. It does not support a universal impossibility claim or a
conclusion about richer directional forgetting families.

\paragraph{Our contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Boundary results rather than a representation mechanism.} We make the assumptions
    behind $f=1$ optimality explicit and derive an exact finite-horizon
    directional condition for beneficial fixed-factor forgetting under an
    isotropic model.  We also give a two-world non-identifiability example for
    unrestricted train--test covariance shift. These are scoped boundary
    analyses, not a predictive theory of anisotropic DINOv2 features.
  \item \textbf{An auditable, class-conditional opportunity--selection decomposition.} We define
    test-swept factors only as diagnostic references within an explicit
    candidate family and evaluate
    train-only choices separately by factor error, matched-risk gain, regret
    and downstream utility.
  \item \textbf{Two retained development diagnostics.} Across five dependent
    splits, the reliability proxy fails its frozen JHU/QNRF material-decision
    gate, while the fixed-factor predictor
    has moderate oracle association but worse factor MAE and test utility than
    constant $f=1$. We retain these reproducible case studies without treating
    them as inferential replications.
  \item \textbf{A held-out learned-selector stress test and matched baselines.}
    A separately preregistered simulator study trains on 512 tasks, selects
    hyperparameters on 128 and evaluates once on 256 disjoint tasks. The
    learned KNN rule has positive held-out utility but does not beat the frozen
    analytic rule on opportunity-subset regret, so the composite success
    criterion is not met. SIFt-RLS and DOS-ELM-style
    results enter the same synthetic head, data, information and risk protocol,
    with their unequal update costs reported explicitly; this synthetic test
    does not resolve directional forgetting on natural DINOv2 features.
  \item \textbf{A single-shot external boundary.} On six reviewed scenes from
    one previously unseen FDST source, the frozen fixed-factor Oracle selects
    $f=1$ and neither train-only rule improves it. This is a
    no-material-opportunity boundary within the frozen scalar grid, not six
    independent replications and not evidence that other datasets or weighting
    families cannot benefit.
  \item \textbf{Explicit evidence boundaries.} Dependent orders and seeds are
    not treated as independent datasets.  Label-defined and non-semantic proxy
    domains do not count as natural-domain replications, and no result is
    promoted beyond its tracked provenance status.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{Analytic continual learning.}
ACIL~\citep{zhuang2022acil} established that recursive feature
accumulation with $f=1$ makes class-incremental learning equivalent to
joint offline training. RanPAC~\citep{mcdonnell2023ranpac} extends this
with random projections for kernel approximation.
DS-AL and GACL~\citep{yang2024dsal,gao2024gacl} scale to larger class
sets. These methods target classification and use absolute memorization.
Recent Geometry-Spectral Rectification (GSR) work applies a data-dependent
anisotropic spectral filter to long-tailed analytic class-incremental learning
\citep{tran2026spectral}.  It addresses Gram-matrix conditioning in
classification rather than selecting historical weights for regression.
They motivate our matched $f=1$ reference, but do not establish that the
same weighting is optimal for heterogeneous visual regression.

\paragraph{Lifelong crowd counting.}
FLCB~\citep{flcb2022} introduced a domain-incremental self-distillation
benchmark for lifelong crowd counting.  It uses end-to-end density
regression with old-model constraints rather than a frozen representation
and closed-form head.
Our setting differs fundamentally: we freeze the backbone and use a
closed-form analytic head, making our update one pass per domain with no
backpropagation. This places us in the analytic CL tradition rather than
the deep distillation tradition.

\paragraph{Continual blind IQA.}
Several works study continual learning for blind image quality assessment
(BIQA). BIQA-CL~\citep{zhang2023biqa_cl} appends one prediction head per
dataset, regularizes the heads during sequential backpropagation, and uses an
adaptive weighted sum at inference. LIQA~\citep{liqa2022} instead generates
pseudo-features for old distortions and applies split-and-merge distillation;
its final regressor is a single-head, task-agnostic model. TSN-IQA
~\citep{chen2024tsn_iqa} freezes convolution filters but learns task-specific
normalization parameters and prediction heads, then combines their outputs
with a lightweight K-means gating mechanism. SILF~\citep{ma2023silf} uses
dynamic parameter isolation, cross-task reuse, and selective pruning under a
memory budget. These are essential task-specific comparators, not variants of
one common multi-head design.  Our method differs in using a single shared
closed-form ridge head without backpropagation or pseudo-feature replay, while
remaining task-aware because the domain-specific target scale $s_k$ is needed
at inference.

\paragraph{Adaptive forgetting in RLS and analytic output layers.}
Variable forgetting factor RLS (VFF-RLS)~\citep{leung1993vff} and
directional forgetting~\citep{kulhavy1987directional} adapt $f$ to
track non-stationary signals in control and signal processing.
Adaptive-forgetting OS-ELM~\citep{lim2013affoselm} and DOS-ELM
~\citep{cao2019doselm} are especially close mathematically: they combine
fixed/random features, an RLS-type analytic output layer, and a dynamically
adjusted forgetting factor, including regression experiments.  A concept
drift map has also been used to regulate OS-ELM forgetting
~\citep{yu2019driftmap}.  Therefore, our contribution is not the invention of
adaptive forgetting for a closed-form regression head, nor a claim that the
present selector is reliable.  It is a controlled, domain-balanced
falsification study of scalar train-only sufficient-statistic selection under
task-aware domain-incremental regression, with natural-feature claims limited
to the frozen crowd pipeline.
Bayesian controlled-forgetting methods provide another adjacent line:
MESU truncates a variational posterior to a finite memory window
\citep{bonnet2025bayesian}, while a recent unreviewed BayesForget preprint
tempers sequential posteriors with a prescribed forgetting schedule
\citep{vallverdu2026bayesian}.  Both update Bayesian neural networks under
streaming drift; neither studies a train-only selector for the shared
closed-form ridge head and balanced external-domain risk considered here.
SIFt-RLS~\citep{lai2024sift} further shows that scalar exponential
forgetting can be too coarse by forgetting only in the incoming information
subspace.  It is an important directional alternative, but a computationally
matched dense-head comparison remains outside the present evidence set.
For the selector-v2 synthetic matched-head comparison, we apply the published
DOS-ELM factor update to the same generated task stream and ridge head.
Because the original paper does
not unambiguously define its regression ``accuracy'' signal, our explicitly
labeled regression adaptation uses negative normalized training RMSE (higher
is better).  Its weighted-design formulation multiplies old design rows by
$\lambda$, so the corresponding old sufficient statistics are multiplied by
$\lambda^2$.  We implement SIFt-RLS directly from its information-matrix
update and select its single fixed factor using training-only validation.
When the incoming regressor spans the entire parameter space, SIFt-RLS
reduces exactly to ordinary exponential forgetting; we record this rank
diagnostic for every domain.
A fixed-$f$ oracle is \emph{not} a VFF-RLS method.  We therefore include a
domain-batched implementation of the robust error/noise-power rule of
\citet{paleologu2008vff}.  Its original rule is sample-wise;
our adaptation estimates the same powers on held-out samples from the
incoming known domain and applies one factor at that boundary.  We report
this adaptation explicitly rather than presenting it as an exact reproduction
of the original streaming system-identification protocol.

\paragraph{Continual regression.}
CLeaR~\citep{he2021clear} provides an adaptive buffered framework for
non-stationary regression, while \citet{zhao2024statistical}
analyze regularization-based continual learning on sequences of linear
regression tasks.  \citet{evron2026rates} connect realizable continual linear
regression to last-iterate SGD and derive random-order forgetting rates;
\citet{levinstein2025optimal} show that fixed or increasing isotropic
regularization can improve those rates under random task orderings.
\citet{karpel2026optimal} derive
high-dimensional generalization expressions and the scaling of optimal
isotropic $L_2$ regularization across continual linear-regression tasks.  This
theoretical line clarifies how regularization changes continual-regression
risk under realizability or distributional assumptions, but it does not
select a forgetting factor or test the decision utility of a train-only
selector under heterogeneous external risk.  EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}
and LwF~\citep{li2017lwf}
can also be adapted to regression; they require backpropagation and,
respectively, Fisher-state or old-model predictions, but not necessarily
stored exemplars. Our setting is narrower: frozen visual features, a shared
closed-form head, known domain boundaries, and no raw-example replay.

\paragraph{Adjacent coefficient selection in model merging.}
Model merging studies how to combine task-specific checkpoints rather than
how to discount temporal sufficient statistics, but it provides important
prior art for information-limited weighting.  \citet{xu2025prodistill}
prove that data-agnostic mergers can have
arbitrarily poor worst-case performance and use few-shot domain data for
distillation.  Weight Weaving~\citep{chaves2026weight} highlights the dependence
of scaling factors on privileged evaluation data and pools parameters across
candidate scales instead of selecting one.  DF-Merge~\citep{lee2025dfmerge}
optimizes non-uniform coefficients with a small labeled validation set.
Conversely, APGD~\citep{wei2025apgd}, NAN~\citep{si2025nan} and
ACE-Merging~\citep{xu2026ace} construct training-free or parameter-only
coefficient/statistical rules, while SVC~\citep{li2026svc} corrects spectral
over-accumulation without data.  These works rule out any broad claim that
source-side information is uniformly insufficient or that coefficient choice
has not been studied.  Our claim is restricted to two prespecified rules and
their permitted statistics in task-aware analytic continual regression; model
merging is an adjacent setting, not evidence for the present result.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Problem Setting}

\paragraph{Task-aware domain-incremental regression.}
We adopt the \emph{task-aware domain-incremental} (TADIL) setting: the
domain identity $k$ is known during both training and inference, but
original data from previous domains is unavailable after processing.
This models deployments with restricted raw-data retention.  It does not,
by itself, provide a formal privacy guarantee: sufficient statistics may
still leak information without an explicit privacy mechanism.
We make no assumption of a shared feature-to-target mapping across domains;
each domain may have its own optimal regressor $w_k$.

Formally, we observe a sequence of $T$ domains $k=1,\dots,T$.
Domain $k$ provides $n_k$ samples $\{(x_i, y_i)\}$ where
$x_i \in \R^d$ are features extracted by a \emph{frozen} vision
backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B/14, $d=768$) and $y_i$ is an image- or patch-level
scalar target.  The reported development evidence instantiates this setting
with total and patch-level crowd counts.
We assume a linear model per domain: $y = x^\top w_k + \varepsilon_k$
where $\varepsilon_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_k^2)$.
After processing domain $k$, the original data is discarded---no exemplar
replay, no access to previous raw data.

\subsection{Recursive Ridge Regression with Forgetting}

The closed-form continual update accumulates second-order sufficient
statistics with a per-step forgetting factor $f_k \in (0,1]$:
\begin{align}
  R_k &= f_k R_{k-1} + X_k^\top X_k, \label{eq:R}\\
  C_k &= f_k C_{k-1} + X_k^\top y_k, \label{eq:C}\\
  W_k &= (R_k + \lambda I)^{-1} C_k. \label{eq:W}
\end{align}
Setting $f_k=1$ for all $k$ recovers the joint ridge solution
(Proposition~\ref{prop:fstar1}).
Setting $f_k<1$ exponentially down-weights older domains.

\paragraph{Class-conditional opportunity.}
Every Oracle statement is indexed by a predeclared candidate family
$\mathcal F$. For test risk $J_{\rm test}$, we report
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathcal F}=
  \frac{J_{\rm test}(1)-\min_{f\in\mathcal F}J_{\rm test}(f)}
       {J_{\rm test}(1)}.
  \label{eq:class_opportunity}
\end{equation}
For the crowd and FDST experiments, $\mathcal F$ is the frozen finite grid of
scalar factors. Thus ``no material opportunity'' means no material improvement
within that grid. It is neither an upper bound nor a null result for a larger
family with per-domain, directional or matrix-valued weights.

\paragraph{Crowd dual-head prediction.}
Each $518{\times}518$ image yields $37{\times}37=1369$ DINOv2 patch
tokens.  A patch ridge head regresses a DCT-5-smoothed patch-count target,
while a second image head regresses total count from the mean-pooled token.
The final count is
$0.25\sum_p\hat y_p+0.75\hat y_{\mathrm{image}}$.
A ridge parameter $\lambda=100$ is used throughout. These frozen pipeline
choices are not selected by the reported test results, but their sensitivity
is not evaluated; all natural-data conclusions are conditional on them.

\paragraph{Memory and computational complexity.}
Each deployed ridge head stores one augmented $d{\times}d$ matrix $R$ and
one $d{\times}1$ vector $C$ (crowd counting deploys two such heads).  The
selector stores one accumulated fitting
quadratic and one accumulated balanced-validation quadratic, rather than a
matrix for every historical domain.  Its total state is therefore $O(d^2)$,
independent of $T$.  With float64 and an augmented dimension of 769, each
quadratic matrix occupies approximately 4.73\,MB.
Per-domain update cost is $O(n_k d^2)$ for accumulation and $O(d^3)$ for
solving; selecting from $B$ globally enumerated factors requires $B$ linear
solves and adds $O(Bd^3)$ at a boundary ($B$ is fixed and independent of $T$).
Critically, \emph{no GPU is required} for the RLS update or $f$ selection;
only the DINOv2 forward pass benefits from GPU acceleration.

\subsection{Magnitude Normalization}
\label{sec:norm}

Crowd counts vary substantially across datasets (SHB: mean~123,
JHU: mean~372, SHA: mean~541), a ${\sim}4\times$ spread.
When this scale mismatch dominates, a forgetting factor $f<1$ merely
compensates for magnitude differences rather than residual negative transfer.
To isolate the two mechanisms, we optionally normalize targets per domain
by the domain training mean before accumulation:
$\tilde{y}_{ki} = y_{ki} / \mu_k$, where $\mu_k =
\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_i y_{ki}$.
This normalization is applied consistently to both $R$ and $C$ updates
and inverted at prediction time.

\subsection{Adaptive Forgetting Selector}
\label{sec:adaptive}

At each domain boundary, we select $f_k$ using a held-out validation procedure
over within-domain
sufficient statistics:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Split:} Divide domain $k$'s training data into deterministic
    80/20 fit and held-out partitions. For KADID, complete reference-image
    groups rather than individual distortions are held out.
  \item \textbf{Fit sufficient statistics:}
    $R^{\mathrm{fit}}_k = X_{\mathrm{fit}}^\top X_{\mathrm{fit}}$,
    $C^{\mathrm{fit}}_k = X_{\mathrm{fit}}^\top y_{\mathrm{fit}}$.
  \item \textbf{Candidate objective:} For $f$ in a predeclared grid
    $\mathcal F\subset[f_{\min},1]$, form
    $R(f) = f\,\bar R^{\mathrm{fit}}_{k-1} + R^{\mathrm{fit}}_k$,
    $C(f) = f\,\bar C^{\mathrm{fit}}_{k-1} + C^{\mathrm{fit}}_k$,
    $W(f) = (R(f)+\lambda I)^{-1} C(f)$.
  \item \textbf{Balanced validation:} Domain $j$ contributes the normalized
    validation MSE
    $[W^\top R_j^vW-2W^\top C_j^v+S_j^v]/n_j^v$.
    Its average needs only the running sums
    $\sum_jR_j^v/n_j^v$, $\sum_jC_j^v/n_j^v$ and
    $\sum_jS_j^v/n_j^v$.  We enumerate the complete grid and retain the
    global grid minimizer. A continuous refinement is only an ablation because
    $\hat J_k(f)$ has not been proven unimodal.
  \item \textbf{Deploy and update state:} Update the deployed head using
    \emph{all} of domain $k$'s data and the selected $f_k^*$. Separately,
    update the selector's fitting state as
    $\bar R_k^{\mathrm{fit}}=f_k^*\bar R_{k-1}^{\mathrm{fit}}+
    R_k^{\mathrm{fit}}$; held-out samples remain only in the balanced
    validation state.
\end{enumerate}

The observable validation-gain score is the relative validation-risk reduction
\begin{equation}
  G_k =
  \frac{\hat J_k(1)-\min_{f\in\mathcal F}\hat J_k(f)}
       {\max(|\hat J_k(1)|,\epsilon)}.
  \label{eq:validation_gain}
\end{equation}
When $G_k$ is below a predeclared numerical tolerance, the selector
abstains and uses $f_k=1$.  This score and $f_k^*$ are computed from the same
train-only objective. It measures actionable validation improvement, not a
physically identified concept-drift magnitude.

\subsection{Held-Out Learned Selector and Matched Forgetting Baselines}
\label{sec:selector-v2-method}

To test whether learning across tasks changes the behavior of the two frozen
scalar rules, protocol selector-v2 defines a disjoint simulator task
distribution within the same scalar-factor candidate family.
For each task, a train-only summary concatenates ordered domain-level local
ridge norms, held-out training residuals, target variances, sample-covariance
trace and condition, plus pairwise coefficient disagreement, cosine and
covariance-weighted disagreement. Standardization is fitted on 512 meta-train
tasks only. Given a new summary, a KNN curve selector retrieves $k$ meta-train
tasks, averages their $f=1$-normalized population-risk curves, and selects the
largest factor attaining the minimum predicted risk. Population curves are
privileged meta-training labels; deployment on a held-out task consumes only
its training summary. The neighbor count is selected on 128 meta-validation
tasks before any of 256 meta-test tasks is generated.

The same protocol includes SIFt-RLS and a DOS-ELM-style regression adaptation.
SIFt applies its information-subspace update to each training row. A candidate
domain-equivalent factor $f_d$ is converted mechanically to
$f_s=f_d^{1/n}$ per sample; one $f_d$ is selected on meta-validation and then
frozen. DOS-ELM applies the published design-row scaling interpretation, so
historical sufficient statistics receive the squared factor, and the current
domain's train-validation score changes only the next domain's factor. These
baselines share the linear head, training stream, ridge, information
permission and population balanced-MSE evaluation. SIFt uses 384 rank-one
updates per task whereas the domain-batched methods use four; this is a
matched statistical setting, not a matched-FLOP claim or a natural-feature
test of directional forgetting.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}
% ==============================================================

The propositions below are boundary analyses for scalar forgetting. They
explain which assumptions are stronger than the informal phrase ``no drift''
and supply a local diagnostic or a scoped counterexample. They are not used to
claim that an isotropic formula predicts the behavior of anisotropic DINOv2
features.

\subsection{Setup and Notation}

Under an isotropic approximation $\E[xx^\top] = s_k I$, define domain
information $\mu_k := n_k s_k$ and age $\tau_k := T-k$.  For analysis,
first consider a common factor $f$ across a finite horizon.  Then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    W(f)&=\frac{\sum_k f^{\tau_k}\mu_kw_k+\sum_kf^{\tau_k}\nu_k}
                 {D(f)},\\
    D(f)&:=\sum_kf^{\tau_k}\mu_k+\lambda .
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Writing $\bar W(f)=\E[W(f)]$ and
$V(f)=\sum_kf^{2\tau_k}\mu_kr_k/D(f)^2$, the balanced excess risk is
\begin{equation}
  J(f)=\frac1T\sum_k\rho_k\norm{\bar W(f)-w_k}^2
       +d\bar\rho V(f).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[A precise no-drift boundary]
\label{prop:fstar1}
Assume (i) $w_k=w$ for every domain, (ii) $\mu_k=\mu$,
(iii) $r_k=r$, and (iv) $\lambda=0$.  Then $f=1$ minimizes expected
prediction risk.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The estimator is unbiased.  With $a_k=f^{\tau_k}$, its variance is
proportional to $\sum_ka_k^2/(\sum_ka_k)^2$.  Cauchy--Schwarz lower-bounds
this quantity by $1/T$, with equality exactly when all $a_k$ are equal.
For $f\in(0,1]$, equality is attained at $f=1$.
\end{proof}

No mapping drift alone is therefore not sufficient outside these conditions.
Unequal noise, unequal information, or non-negligible ridge bias can make
$f<1$ beneficial even when all $w_k$ coincide; such a gain must not be labeled
as concept-drift evidence.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-horizon directional condition]
\label{prop:fstar_lt1}
At $f=1$, define $D=M+\lambda$, $N=\sum_k\mu_kw_k$,
$Q=\sum_k\mu_kr_k$, and
$D'=\sum_k\tau_k\mu_k$, $N'=\sum_k\tau_k\mu_kw_k$,
$Q'=\sum_k2\tau_k\mu_kr_k$.  Let
\begin{align}
 \bar W' &= (N'D-ND')/D^2,\\
 V' &= Q'/D^2-2QD'/D^3.
\end{align}
If
\begin{equation}
 J'_-(1)=\frac2T\sum_k\rho_k(\bar W(1)-w_k)^\top\bar W'
          +d\bar\rho V' >0,
 \label{eq:suff_cond}
\end{equation}
then every minimizer over $(0,1]$ satisfies $f^*<1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the finite sums defining $\bar W(f)=N(f)/D(f)$ and
$V(f)=Q(f)/D(f)^2$.  If the left derivative at one is positive, moving to a
sufficiently nearby smaller factor strictly reduces $J$, so one cannot be a
minimizer.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:suff_cond} retains the exact finite-horizon variance
derivative.  The implemented validation-gain score $G_k$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:validation_gain} is an empirical decision statistic for the
actual per-boundary objective; it is not an exact estimator of the derivative
or of $f^*$.

\begin{proposition}[Scoped train-only non-identifiability]
\label{prop:train_only_nonidentifiability}
Let $\mathcal I_{\rm tr}$ contain the complete training samples, deterministic
split identities and every sufficient statistic computed from them.  Without
an assumption linking training and test covariances, no deterministic rule
$S(\mathcal I_{\rm tr})$ can always select the balanced-test-risk-optimal
fixed factor, even for two scalar noiseless linear domains with equal training
information and $\lambda=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use identical training data in two worlds, with domain regressors $w_1=0$ and
$w_2=1$ and equal training information.  Hence both worlds have the same
$\mathcal I_{\rm tr}$ and produce
$W(f)=1/(1+f)$.  Their balanced test risk is
\begin{equation}
  L(f)=\tfrac12\{\rho_1[W(f)-w_1]^2+
                         \rho_2[W(f)-w_2]^2\},
\end{equation}
where $\rho_k=\E_{\rm test}[x_k^2]$.  In World A, let
$(\rho_1,\rho_2)=(1,1)$; the risk-optimal prediction is $1/2$, so
$f_A^*=1$.  In World B, let $(\rho_1,\rho_2)=(1,4)$; the risk-optimal
prediction is $4/5$, so $f_B^*=1/4$.  A deterministic rule sees identical
training information in both worlds and must return the same factor, which
cannot equal both optima.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:train_only_nonidentifiability} is deliberately narrow.
It concerns target-domain covariance information absent from
$\mathcal I_{\rm tr}$ under unrestricted train--test covariate shift.  It does
not imply failure under an i.i.d. train--test assumption, does not rule out
other source-side structural assumptions, and does not preclude a disjoint
target-calibration set from identifying the relevant risk weights.  Unlike a
general worst-case theorem for data-agnostic model merging, it fixes the RLS
model, loss, two-domain sequence and information permission used here.

change and covariance anisotropy. The primary loss is population balanced
normalized MSE; the reverse order is a sensitivity check and is averaged
within seed before the paired-seed bootstrap. The fixed-$f$ Oracle sweeps the

\paragraph{What the protocol records.}
The implemented score is $G_k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:validation_gain}: the
relative reduction of a balanced held-out quadratic objective. Each result
artifact schema stores $G_k$, $\hat J_k(1)$, the selected objective, and the complete
factor choice at every boundary. This is designed to make the selector's
decision auditable without test labels when the original artifacts are
available. We deliberately do not rename $G_k$ a ``concept-drift
magnitude,'' because label noise, unequal information, ridge bias, and
representation error can also change it.

\paragraph{Scope of the propositions.}
Propositions~\ref{prop:fstar1}--\ref{prop:fstar_lt1} analyze a common fixed
factor under an isotropic finite-horizon model. The deployed algorithm chooses
a causal sequence $(f_2,\ldots,f_T)$, under which domain $j$ receives weight
$\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{T}f_\ell$. The propositions explain why $f=1$ needs
stronger conditions than ``no mapping drift'' and provide a local directional
diagnostic, but they are not a regret or optimality guarantee for the greedy
selector. The fixed-factor derived table above indicates that the isotropic
prediction is not calibrated well enough for numerical selection claims; its
original artifact package has been restored and independently recomputed.

\paragraph{Why a local pair score is insufficient.}
The candidate model at boundary $k$ depends on the entire accumulated
sufficient-statistic state, so two identical adjacent-domain pairs can imply
different optimal discounts when their earlier histories differ. This
structural observation motivates evaluating candidate factors against the
balanced history rather than mapping a pairwise feature distance directly to
$f_k$.  An earlier numerical pairwise study lacked the same data-identity and
provenance checks and is therefore excluded from the present claim set rather
than retained as evidence.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ==============================================================

\paragraph{Isotropic approximation.}
Propositions~1--2 rely on $\Sigma_k = s_k I$; real DINOv2 features are
non-isotropic, and the common-$f$ analysis does not guarantee the causal
per-boundary selector.  The tracked calibration-derived table indicates that
this is a material rather than cosmetic limitation: its selected factor is
worse than constant $f=1$ in both factor MAE and average test risk.  An anisotropic
analysis and a selector stability or regret bound remain open. Accordingly,
the propositions support boundary statements about sufficient conditions and
effective sample size, not a mechanism claim or calibrated prediction for
DINOv2.

\paragraph{Controlled-model scope.}
Synthetic-v1 uses balanced domain risk, an exactly specified linear
generator and i.i.d. train/test samples.  Its 16-cell null result establishes
that the preregistered factor switches are not sufficient for a material
opportunity within the frozen scalar grid in that model; it does not show that other
amplitudes, asymmetric deployment mixtures, structured shifts or nonlinear
representations cannot create one.  Conversely, the harmful
reliability--mapping interactions are controlled counterexamples, not a claim
that precision weighting is universally harmful.

Selector-v2 deliberately broadens the simulator and supplies a cross-task
prior through Oracle-labeled meta-training curves. Its positive held-out KNN
utility therefore does not contradict the unrestricted non-identifiability
example, and its unmet composite criterion does not prove that richer learned
selectors cannot succeed. The 40-task opportunity subset is defined by a
diagnostic Oracle and is reported only because the subset rule was frozen
before meta-test. Neither synthetic protocol establishes natural-image
generalization.

\paragraph{Candidate-family scope.}
Equation~\ref{eq:class_opportunity} measures improvement only over the frozen
scalar grid. A zero value therefore cannot establish that per-domain,
directional, spectral or matrix-valued weighting lacks opportunity. Even a
positive value is a lower bound on the best improvement available to a larger
family, not an upper bound on historical reweighting in general.

\paragraph{Scope of the non-identifiability example.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:train_only_nonidentifiability} allows unrestricted
domain-specific train--test covariance shift.  Its two worlds show only that
the missing test-risk weights cannot be recovered from identical training
information without an additional link between distributions.  The example
does not cover an i.i.d. train--test setting, structured shift assumptions,
randomized rules with weaker guarantees, or methods that use a disjoint target
calibration set.

\paragraph{Natural domains and replication.}
AVA score tiers and UTK age bands are label-defined; NYU source batches are
proxy domains and cannot establish natural-domain generalization.  FDST adds
one previously unseen, non-label-defined source under a frozen single-shot
protocol, but its scalar-grid Oracle opportunity is absent. It therefore
provides a natural-data class-conditional boundary rather than a replication
of the development-set opportunity--selection gap. Six selected scenes do not become six independent
replications, and the result does not establish behavior on other video,
backbone or deployment distributions.

\paragraph{Linear head capacity and directional forgetting.}
Our experiments use scalar exponential forgetting in a linear ridge head.
Random-feature expansion changes both capacity and storage and does not
replace a matched directional-forgetting comparison. Selector-v2 now includes
clean SIFt-RLS and DOS-ELM-style results with the same synthetic linear head,
data, information and risk, but SIFt performs many more updates. These results
do not constitute a FLOP-matched comparison and do not test the full
dual-head crowd model. A natural-data, computationally matched treatment of
the dense patch head remains necessary. We therefore do not infer that
directional forgetting fails from the scalar-rule results.

\paragraph{Frozen pipeline hyperparameters.}
The natural-data pipeline fixes DCT-5 target smoothing, dual-head mixture
$0.25/0.75$ and ridge $\lambda=100$. We do not report a preregistered
sensitivity analysis over these choices. The crowd and FDST results are thus
conditional on this pipeline; changing these values after observing the
single-shot result would be exploratory rather than independent confirmation.

\paragraph{Development-set resolution.}
C4 uses five dependent development splits and a $4/5$ decision gate. A single
split reversal changes the gate, and the 40 fixed-factor scenarios also reuse
datasets and orders. We retain these cases for reproducible failure diagnosis,
do not attach i.i.d. confidence intervals, and do not treat them as a
population-frequency estimate. The disjoint 256-task selector-v2 test supplies
stronger synthetic uncertainty quantification but no natural-data replication.

\paragraph{Deep distillation baselines.}
We do not fully implement FLCB's deep distillation variant, which
operates in a different computational budget (end-to-end training,
old-model constraints). Comparison is therefore at the ``closed-form
analytic CL'' level, not against deep lifelong methods.

\paragraph{Metric alignment and uncertainty.}
The selector optimizes normalized MSE, whereas some headline summaries use
relative MAE.  The fixed-factor calibration finds configurations where the
matched-MSE and relative-MAE effects have opposite signs; overall, the
selected factor is harmful under both metrics. Final evaluation must continue
to report both metrics, fixed manifests/seeds and paired uncertainty. Small
gains without these checks are not treated as reliable.

\section{Reproducibility and Data Statement}
\label{sec:reproducibility}
% ==============================================================

The anonymous supplement provides the frozen protocols, split metadata,
derived result tables, validation programs and manifest-bearing component
archives used for the reported claims. Selector-v2 additionally provides its
pre-test selection lock, disjoint meta-split records and full held-out task
records. Each archive records file hashes, and
the top-level bundle has a separate SHA-256 checksum. Diagnostic Oracles use
held-out labels only to measure whether opportunity exists within their stated
candidate families; they are not
deployable selectors. The FDST raw videos, images and annotations are not
redistributed. The supplement contains only derived FDST results and the
metadata and code needed to audit the frozen evaluation; access to the source
data remains governed by the dataset authors and the original dataset terms.

% ==============================================================
\section{Broader Impact}
\label{sec:broader-impact}
% ==============================================================

Continual visual regression can support applications such as resource
planning and safety analysis, but crowd-counting systems can also be used in
surveillance settings with privacy, consent and governance risks. This work
does not deploy identity tracking or a monitoring system; it studies frozen
representations, aggregate labels and the reliability of a train-only
selection mechanism. Any downstream deployment should respect dataset and
image licensing, assess consent and proportionality, protect personal data,
and separately test distribution shift and subgroup harms. The negative and
null results reported here should not be read as evidence that a deployed
system is safe or reliable.

% ==============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ==============================================================

This work asks a narrower question than prior drafts: in task-aware
domain-incremental visual regression with frozen foundation features and a
shared ridge head, can train-only balanced sufficient statistics select a
useful factor from a frozen scalar-forgetting family? For the two rules
represented in the natural-data development tables, neither meets its frozen
decision criterion. These low-resolution cases are development diagnostics:
the C4 selector misses or falsely triggers under its material-decision gate,
and the isotropic fixed-factor predictor has worse
factor MAE than constant $f=1$ and negative average test utility.  At the same
time, the matched-loss oracle retains positive opportunity within the fixed
scalar grid, separating class-conditional headroom from the ability to select
it causally. The preregistered controlled study adds a complementary boundary:
under its balanced i.i.d. linear generator, none of 16 cells produces a
material scalar-grid opportunity, even though scale-isolation and
reliability-direction checks pass.  The natural-data claims remain development
findings where an opportunity is claimed.

A separate preregistered simulator study directly tests whether richer rule
design changes that conclusion. Its learned KNN selector improves over $f=1$
on held-out tasks and passes three of four gates, but it does not beat the
frozen analytic rule on opportunity-subset regret. The preregistered composite
success criterion is therefore not met, but the positive KNN utility remains
evidence of a different utility--regret trade-off; neither selector dominates.
Matched SIFt-RLS is neutral overall and DOS-ELM-style is harmful in the limited
8-dimensional synthetic comparison, which does not settle natural-feature
directional forgetting. The single-shot FDST boundary instead finds no
material opportunity within its fixed scalar grid: its Oracle selects $f=1$,
neither frozen train-only rule improves it, and precision weighting is harmful.
Complete
local provenance chains were independently checked, and publication-safe
artifacts are packaged with manifests and hashes.

The contribution is therefore a boundary-analysis and falsification protocol
with derived development findings and a held-out synthetic stress test, not a
validated adaptive algorithm or an algorithmic-superiority claim.
Adaptive-forgetting OS-ELM and directional RLS
are clear prior art.  The theory covers a simplified common-factor model, and
its non-identifiability example depends on unrestricted covariance shift.
The unseen FDST result shows that the development scalar-grid gap does not
automatically generalize under the same frozen pipeline. Other independent
data may test different candidate families and hyperparameters, but
FDST must not be reused to tune another selector after this single-shot result.
