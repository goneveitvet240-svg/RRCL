\begin{abstract}
% ==============================================================
Analytic continual learning updates a recursive least-squares (RLS) head over
frozen features without raw-example replay.  Although $f=1$ exactly recovers
the matched joint ridge solution, uniform historical weighting need not
minimize balanced risk across heterogeneous domains.  We therefore separate
\emph{opportunity}---whether a frozen scalar-factor family contains a useful
$f<1$---from \emph{selection} by a train-only rule.  In an isotropic model we
derive conditions for $f=1$, a two-domain optimum that interprets forgetting as
information rebalancing, and a scoped non-identifiability example under
unrestricted train--test covariance shift.

Four frozen tests bound the empirical claim.  A preregistered $2^4$ controlled
study and a single-shot unseen FDST source show no material opportunity within
their scalar grids.  A broader held-out simulator contains 40 opportunity
tasks among 256: a KNN selector improves over $f=1$ by $0.520\%$ on this subset,
but has higher Oracle regret than the frozen analytic rule ($0.701\%$ versus
$0.429\%$), so the preregistered composite criterion is not met.  Two
low-resolution crowd-counting development diagnostics likewise retain missed
or harmful selections despite positive diagnostic Oracle headroom.  The
evidence therefore supports neither always using $f=1$ nor a validated
adaptive selector: useful scalar discounting exists in some conditions, but
the tested train-only rules do not identify it reliably.  The conclusion is
restricted to the studied scalar families, grids and task distributions; it
is not a universal train-only impossibility or a claim about directional
forgetting.
\end{abstract}

% ==============================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ==============================================================

Large pretrained vision models such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2023dinov2} and
CLIP~\citep{radford2021learning} provide transferable frozen features.  A
closed-form ridge head can then be updated without backpropagation or raw-
example replay.  Analytic continual-learning methods such as
ACIL~\citep{zhuang2022acil}, RanPAC~\citep{mcdonnell2023ranpac},
DS-AL~\citep{yang2024dsal} and GACL~\citep{gao2024gacl} exploit the fact that
accumulating all sufficient statistics with $f=1$ is algebraically equivalent
to fitting the matched joint head.  This identity specifies a strong and
reproducible reference; it is not a task-performance upper bound.  When
domains differ in information, noise, label scale or feature-to-target
mapping, a balanced multi-domain objective need not prefer the same weights
as pooled empirical risk.

Visual regression makes this distinction explicit because errors are
continuous and domain scales can differ substantially.  A test sweep may show
that down-weighting some historical domains could improve balanced risk, but
such an oracle does not define a deployable rule.  A causal learner must decide
from its permitted training-side state, without consuming test labels or
retroactively changing the rule.  We therefore ask two linked but distinct
questions: \textbf{when does a predeclared scalar-factor family contain useful
non-uniform historical weighting, and can a prespecified train-only statistic
select it with positive decision utility?}

We answer these questions in task-aware analytic continual visual regression
with a frozen DINOv2 representation and shared ridge head.  Our development
protocol retains two negative outcomes: a reliability-reweighting rule fails
its material-decision gate, and an isotropic fixed-factor predictor is
moderately correlated with a test oracle but performs worse than the constant
$f=1$ rule when deployed. This pattern motivates a class-conditional
distinction between weighting headroom and selection from a restricted
information set. It does not support a universal impossibility claim or a
conclusion about richer directional forgetting families.

\paragraph{Our contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Theory.} We state exact conditions for $f=1$, derive the
    two-domain optimum $f^\star=\min\{1,(1+c)/(a+c)\}$, and give a scoped
    train-only non-identifiability example.  The analysis treats forgetting as
    information rebalancing rather than a direct drift-magnitude estimator.
  \item \textbf{Auditable decomposition.} We distinguish Oracle opportunity
    within an explicit scalar family from causal selection, reporting factor
    error, matched-risk gain, regret and downstream utility.
  \item \textbf{Falsification tests.} Controlled synthetic, held-out learned-
    selector, matched-baseline, FDST and retained development studies delimit
    when opportunity appears and show that the tested selectors are not
    reliably deployable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ==============================================================

ACIL~\citep{zhuang2022acil} established that recursive feature
accumulation with $f=1$ makes class-incremental learning equivalent to
joint offline training. RanPAC~\citep{mcdonnell2023ranpac} extends this
with random projections for kernel approximation.
DS-AL and GACL~\citep{yang2024dsal,gao2024gacl} scale to larger class
sets. These methods target classification and use absolute memorization.
Recent Geometry-Spectral Rectification (GSR) work applies a data-dependent
anisotropic spectral filter to long-tailed analytic class-incremental learning
\citep{tran2026spectral}.  It addresses Gram-matrix conditioning in
classification rather than selecting historical weights for regression.
They motivate our matched $f=1$ reference, but do not establish that the
same weighting is optimal for heterogeneous visual regression.

FLCB~\citep{flcb2022} introduced a domain-incremental self-distillation
benchmark for lifelong crowd counting.  It uses end-to-end density
regression with old-model constraints rather than a frozen representation
and closed-form head.  Related continual blind-IQA systems use per-dataset
heads, pseudo-feature replay, task-specific normalization, gating or parameter
isolation rather than one shared analytic head
\citep{zhang2023biqa_cl,liqa2022,chen2024tsn_iqa,ma2023silf}.
Several works study continual learning for blind image quality assessment
without the no-replay, closed-form update studied here.

\paragraph{Adaptive forgetting in RLS and analytic output layers.}
Variable forgetting factor RLS (VFF-RLS)~\citep{leung1993vff} and
directional forgetting~\citep{kulhavy1987directional} adapt $f$ to
track non-stationary signals in control and signal processing.
Adaptive-forgetting OS-ELM~\citep{lim2013affoselm} and DOS-ELM
~\citep{cao2019doselm} are especially close mathematically: they combine
fixed/random features, an RLS-type analytic output layer, and a dynamically
adjusted forgetting factor, including regression experiments.  A concept
drift map has also been used to regulate OS-ELM forgetting
~\citep{yu2019driftmap}.  Therefore, our contribution is not the invention of
adaptive forgetting for a closed-form regression head, nor a claim that the
present selector is reliable.  It is a controlled, domain-balanced
falsification study of scalar train-only sufficient-statistic selection under
task-aware domain-incremental regression, with natural-feature claims limited
to the frozen crowd pipeline.
SIFt-RLS~\citep{lai2024sift} further shows that scalar exponential
forgetting can be too coarse by forgetting only in the incoming information
subspace.  Our matched synthetic comparison implements SIFt directly and a
clearly labeled DOS-ELM regression adaptation on the same head and data; update
counts remain unequal.  A domain-batched adaptation of the sample-wise robust
VFF rule of \citet{paleologu2008vff} is reported separately and is not treated
as an exact reproduction.

\paragraph{Continual regression.}
CLeaR~\citep{he2021clear} provides an adaptive buffered framework for
non-stationary regression, while \citet{zhao2024statistical}
analyze regularization-based continual learning on sequences of linear
regression tasks.  \citet{evron2026rates} connect realizable continual linear
regression to last-iterate SGD and derive random-order forgetting rates;
\citet{levinstein2025optimal} show that fixed or increasing isotropic
regularization can improve those rates under random task orderings.
\citet{karpel2026optimal} derive
high-dimensional generalization expressions and the scaling of optimal
isotropic $L_2$ regularization across continual linear-regression tasks.  This
theoretical line clarifies how regularization changes continual-regression
risk under realizability or distributional assumptions, but it does not
select a forgetting factor or test the decision utility of a train-only
selector under heterogeneous external risk.  EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017ewc}
and LwF~\citep{li2017lwf}
can also be adapted to regression; they require backpropagation and,
respectively, Fisher-state or old-model predictions, but not necessarily
stored exemplars. Our setting is narrower: frozen visual features, a shared
closed-form head, known domain boundaries, and no raw-example replay.

Model merging studies how to combine task-specific checkpoints rather than
how to discount temporal sufficient statistics, but it provides important
prior art for information-limited weighting.  \citet{xu2025prodistill}
prove that data-agnostic mergers can have
arbitrarily poor worst-case performance and use few-shot domain data for
distillation.  Weight Weaving~\citep{chaves2026weight} highlights the dependence
of scaling factors on privileged evaluation data and pools parameters across
candidate scales instead of selecting one.  DF-Merge~\citep{lee2025dfmerge}
optimizes non-uniform coefficients with a small labeled validation set.
Conversely, APGD~\citep{wei2025apgd}, NAN~\citep{si2025nan} and
ACE-Merging~\citep{xu2026ace} construct training-free or parameter-only
coefficient/statistical rules, while SVC~\citep{li2026svc} corrects spectral
over-accumulation without data.  These works rule out any broad claim that
source-side information is uniformly insufficient.  Our claim concerns only
the stated RLS model, scalar family, risk and information permission.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% ==============================================================

\subsection{Problem Setting}

\paragraph{Task-aware domain-incremental regression.}
We adopt the \emph{task-aware domain-incremental} (TADIL) setting: the
domain identity $k$ is known during both training and inference, but
original data from previous domains is unavailable after processing.
This models deployments with restricted raw-data retention.  It does not,
by itself, provide a formal privacy guarantee: sufficient statistics may
still leak information without an explicit privacy mechanism.
We make no assumption of a shared feature-to-target mapping across domains;
each domain may have its own optimal regressor $w_k$.

Formally, we observe a sequence of $T$ domains $k=1,\dots,T$.
Domain $k$ provides $n_k$ samples $\{(x_i, y_i)\}$ where
$x_i \in \R^d$ are features extracted by a \emph{frozen} vision
backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B/14, $d=768$) and $y_i$ is an image- or patch-level
scalar target.  The reported development evidence instantiates this setting
with total and patch-level crowd counts.
We assume a linear model per domain: $y = x^\top w_k + \varepsilon_k$
where $\varepsilon_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_k^2)$.
After processing domain $k$, the original data is discarded---no exemplar
replay, no access to previous raw data.

\subsection{Recursive Ridge Regression with Forgetting}

The closed-form continual update accumulates second-order sufficient
statistics with a per-step forgetting factor $f_k \in (0,1]$:
\begin{align}
  R_k &= f_k R_{k-1} + X_k^\top X_k, \label{eq:R}\\
  C_k &= f_k C_{k-1} + X_k^\top y_k, \label{eq:C}\\
  W_k &= (R_k + \lambda I)^{-1} C_k. \label{eq:W}
\end{align}
Setting $f_k=1$ for all $k$ recovers the joint ridge solution
(Proposition~\ref{prop:fstar1}).
Setting $f_k<1$ exponentially down-weights older domains.

\paragraph{Class-conditional opportunity.}
Every Oracle statement is indexed by a predeclared candidate family
$\mathcal F$. For test risk $J_{\rm test}$, we report
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathcal F}=
  \frac{J_{\rm test}(1)-\min_{f\in\mathcal F}J_{\rm test}(f)}
       {J_{\rm test}(1)}.
  \label{eq:class_opportunity}
\end{equation}
For the crowd and FDST experiments, $\mathcal F$ is the frozen finite grid of
scalar factors. Thus ``no material opportunity'' means no material improvement
within that grid. It is neither an upper bound nor a null result for a larger
family with per-domain, directional or matrix-valued weights.

\paragraph{Crowd dual-head prediction.}
Each $518{\times}518$ image yields $37{\times}37=1369$ DINOv2 patch
tokens.  A patch ridge head regresses a DCT-5-smoothed patch-count target,
while a second image head regresses total count from the mean-pooled token.
The final count is
$0.25\sum_p\hat y_p+0.75\hat y_{\mathrm{image}}$.
A ridge parameter $\lambda=100$ is used throughout. These frozen pipeline
choices are not selected by the reported test results, but their sensitivity
is not evaluated; all natural-data conclusions are conditional on them.

\paragraph{Memory and computational complexity.}
Each deployed ridge head stores one augmented $d{\times}d$ matrix $R$ and
one $d{\times}1$ vector $C$ (crowd counting deploys two such heads).  The
selector stores one accumulated fitting
quadratic and one accumulated balanced-validation quadratic, rather than a
matrix for every historical domain.  Its total state is therefore $O(d^2)$,
independent of $T$.  With float64 and an augmented dimension of 769, each
quadratic matrix occupies approximately 4.73\,MB.
Per-domain update cost is $O(n_k d^2)$ for accumulation and $O(d^3)$ for
solving; selecting from $B$ globally enumerated factors requires $B$ linear
solves and adds $O(Bd^3)$ at a boundary ($B$ is fixed and independent of $T$).
Critically, \emph{no GPU is required} for the RLS update or $f$ selection;
only the DINOv2 forward pass benefits from GPU acceleration.

\subsection{Magnitude Normalization}
\label{sec:norm}

Crowd counts vary substantially across datasets (SHB: mean~123,
JHU: mean~372, SHA: mean~541), a ${\sim}4\times$ spread.
When this scale mismatch dominates, a forgetting factor $f<1$ merely
compensates for magnitude differences rather than residual negative transfer.
To isolate the two mechanisms, we optionally normalize targets per domain
by the domain training mean before accumulation:
$\tilde{y}_{ki} = y_{ki} / \mu_k$, where $\mu_k =
\frac{1}{n_k}\sum_i y_{ki}$.
This normalization is applied consistently to both $R$ and $C$ updates
and inverted at prediction time.

\subsection{Adaptive Forgetting Selector}
\label{sec:adaptive}

At each domain boundary, we select $f_k$ using a held-out validation procedure
over within-domain
sufficient statistics:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Split:} Divide domain $k$'s training data into deterministic
    80/20 fit and held-out partitions. For KADID, complete reference-image
    groups rather than individual distortions are held out.
  \item \textbf{Fit sufficient statistics:}
    $R^{\mathrm{fit}}_k = X_{\mathrm{fit}}^\top X_{\mathrm{fit}}$,
    $C^{\mathrm{fit}}_k = X_{\mathrm{fit}}^\top y_{\mathrm{fit}}$.
  \item \textbf{Candidate objective:} For $f$ in a predeclared grid
    $\mathcal F\subset[f_{\min},1]$, form
    $R(f) = f\,\bar R^{\mathrm{fit}}_{k-1} + R^{\mathrm{fit}}_k$,
    $C(f) = f\,\bar C^{\mathrm{fit}}_{k-1} + C^{\mathrm{fit}}_k$,
    $W(f) = (R(f)+\lambda I)^{-1} C(f)$.
  \item \textbf{Balanced validation:} Domain $j$ contributes the normalized
    validation MSE
    $[W^\top R_j^vW-2W^\top C_j^v+S_j^v]/n_j^v$.
    Its average needs only the running sums
    $\sum_jR_j^v/n_j^v$, $\sum_jC_j^v/n_j^v$ and
    $\sum_jS_j^v/n_j^v$.  We enumerate the complete grid and retain the
    global grid minimizer. A continuous refinement is only an ablation because
    $\hat J_k(f)$ has not been proven unimodal.
  \item \textbf{Deploy and update state:} Update the deployed head using
    \emph{all} of domain $k$'s data and the selected $f_k^*$. Separately,
    update the selector's fitting state as
    $\bar R_k^{\mathrm{fit}}=f_k^*\bar R_{k-1}^{\mathrm{fit}}+
    R_k^{\mathrm{fit}}$; held-out samples remain only in the balanced
    validation state.
\end{enumerate}

The observable validation-gain score is the relative validation-risk reduction
\begin{equation}
  G_k =
  \frac{\hat J_k(1)-\min_{f\in\mathcal F}\hat J_k(f)}
       {\max(|\hat J_k(1)|,\epsilon)}.
  \label{eq:validation_gain}
\end{equation}
When $G_k$ is below a predeclared numerical tolerance, the selector
abstains and uses $f_k=1$.  This score and $f_k^*$ are computed from the same
train-only objective. It measures actionable validation improvement, not a
physically identified concept-drift magnitude.

\subsection{Held-Out Learned Selector and Matched Forgetting Baselines}
\label{sec:selector-v2-method}

To test whether learning across tasks changes the behavior of the two frozen
scalar rules, protocol selector-v2 defines a disjoint simulator task
distribution within the same scalar-factor candidate family.
For each task, a train-only summary concatenates ordered domain-level local
ridge norms, held-out training residuals, target variances, sample-covariance
trace and condition, plus pairwise coefficient disagreement, cosine and
covariance-weighted disagreement. Standardization is fitted on 512 meta-train
tasks only. Given a new summary, a KNN curve selector retrieves $k$ meta-train
tasks, averages their $f=1$-normalized population-risk curves, and selects the
largest factor attaining the minimum predicted risk. Population curves are
privileged meta-training labels; deployment on a held-out task consumes only
its training summary. The neighbor count is selected on 128 meta-validation
tasks before any of 256 meta-test tasks is generated.

The same protocol includes SIFt-RLS and a DOS-ELM-style regression adaptation.
SIFt applies its information-subspace update to each training row. A candidate
domain-equivalent factor $f_d$ is converted mechanically to
$f_s=f_d^{1/n}$ per sample; one $f_d$ is selected on meta-validation and then
frozen. DOS-ELM applies the published design-row scaling interpretation, so
historical sufficient statistics receive the squared factor, and the current
domain's train-validation score changes only the next domain's factor. These
baselines share the linear head, training stream, ridge, information
permission and population balanced-MSE evaluation. SIFt uses 384 rank-one
updates per task whereas the domain-batched methods use four; this is a
matched statistical setting, not a matched-FLOP claim or a natural-feature
test of directional forgetting.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}
% ==============================================================

The propositions below are boundary analyses for scalar forgetting. They
explain which assumptions are stronger than the informal phrase ``no drift''
and supply a local diagnostic or a scoped counterexample. They are not used to
claim that an isotropic formula predicts the behavior of anisotropic DINOv2
features.

\subsection{Setup and Notation}

Under an isotropic approximation $\E[xx^\top] = s_k I$, define domain
information $\mu_k := n_k s_k$ and age $\tau_k := T-k$.  For analysis,
first consider a common factor $f$ across a finite horizon.  Then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    W(f)&=\frac{\sum_k f^{\tau_k}\mu_kw_k+\sum_kf^{\tau_k}\nu_k}
                 {D(f)},\\
    D(f)&:=\sum_kf^{\tau_k}\mu_k+\lambda .
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Writing $\bar W(f)=\E[W(f)]$ and
$V(f)=\sum_kf^{2\tau_k}\mu_kr_k/D(f)^2$, the balanced excess risk is
\begin{equation}
  J(f)=\frac1T\sum_k\rho_k\norm{\bar W(f)-w_k}^2
       +d\bar\rho V(f).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[A precise no-drift boundary]
\label{prop:fstar1}
Assume (i) $w_k=w$ for every domain, (ii) $\mu_k=\mu$,
(iii) $r_k=r$, and (iv) $\lambda=0$.  Then $f=1$ minimizes expected
prediction risk.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The estimator is unbiased.  With $a_k=f^{\tau_k}$, its variance is
proportional to $\sum_ka_k^2/(\sum_ka_k)^2$.  Cauchy--Schwarz lower-bounds
this quantity by $1/T$, with equality exactly when all $a_k$ are equal.
For $f\in(0,1]$, equality is attained at $f=1$.
\end{proof}

No mapping drift alone is therefore not sufficient outside these conditions.
Unequal noise, unequal information, or non-negligible ridge bias can make
$f<1$ beneficial even when all $w_k$ coincide; such a gain must not be labeled
as concept-drift evidence.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-horizon directional condition]
\label{prop:fstar_lt1}
At $f=1$, define $D=M+\lambda$, $N=\sum_k\mu_kw_k$,
$Q=\sum_k\mu_kr_k$, and
$D'=\sum_k\tau_k\mu_k$, $N'=\sum_k\tau_k\mu_kw_k$,
$Q'=\sum_k2\tau_k\mu_kr_k$.  Let
\begin{align}
 \bar W' &= (N'D-ND')/D^2,\\
 V' &= Q'/D^2-2QD'/D^3.
\end{align}
If
\begin{equation}
 J'_-(1)=\frac2T\sum_k\rho_k(\bar W(1)-w_k)^\top\bar W'
          +d\bar\rho V' >0,
 \label{eq:suff_cond}
\end{equation}
then every minimizer over $(0,1]$ satisfies $f^*<1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate the finite sums defining $\bar W(f)=N(f)/D(f)$ and
$V(f)=Q(f)/D(f)^2$.  If the left derivative at one is positive, moving to a
sufficiently nearby smaller factor strictly reduces $J$, so one cannot be a
minimizer.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:suff_cond} retains the exact finite-horizon variance
derivative.  The implemented validation-gain score $G_k$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:validation_gain} is an empirical decision statistic for the
actual per-boundary objective; it is not an exact estimator of the derivative
or of $f^*$.

Propositions~\ref{prop:fstar1}--\ref{prop:fstar_lt1} say when $f=1$ stops being
optimal but not what the optimum is a function of.  Specializing the same
isotropic model to two domains makes that dependence explicit.

\begin{corollary}[Two-domain optimum as information rebalancing]
\label{cor:information_balance}
Let domain $1$ carry $n_1$ samples with coefficient $w_1$ and domain $2$ carry
$n_2$ samples with $w_2=w_1+\Delta$. Assume idealized isotropic sufficient
statistics $R_k=n_kI$, independent cross-statistic noise with covariance
$n_k\sigma^2I$, weights $(f,1)$ and $\lambda=0$. For $\Delta\ne0$, write
$a=n_1/n_2>0$ for the information ratio and
$c=2d\sigma^2/(\norm{\Delta}^2n_2)$ for the noise-to-mismatch ratio. Then the
balanced population excess risk, averaged over training noise, is
\begin{equation}
  L(f)=\frac{\tfrac12\norm{\Delta}^2\big[(1+f^2a^2)+c\,(f^2a+1)\big]}
             {(fa+1)^2},
  \label{eq:two_domain_risk}
\end{equation}
the unconstrained positive stationary point is $(1+c)/(a+c)$, and the
constrained minimizer over $0<f\le1$ is
\begin{equation}
  f^\star=\min\Big\{1,\ \frac{1+c}{a+c}\Big\}.
  \label{eq:two_domain_fstar}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
With $\lambda=0$ and $D=fn_1+n_2$ the estimator is
$W(f)=(fn_1w_1+n_2w_2)/D$ plus mean-zero noise, so
$W-w_1=n_2\Delta/D$ and $W-w_2=-fn_1\Delta/D$, giving squared bias
$\tfrac12\norm{\Delta}^2(n_2^2+f^2n_1^2)/D^2$ and variance
$d\sigma^2(f^2n_1+n_2)/D^2$.  Dividing through by $n_2^2$ yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:two_domain_risk}.  Differentiating,
$L'(f)\propto 2a(fa+1)\big[f(a+c)-(1+c)\big]$, whose only positive root is
$f=(1+c)/(a+c)$. The bracket is negative below it and positive above. If
$a>1$ the root lies in $(0,1)$; if $a=1$ it equals one; and if $a<1$ it lies
above one, making the constrained minimizer the boundary $f=1$.
\end{proof}

Two limits carry the interpretation. For $\Delta=0$, where $c$ is undefined,
directly minimizing the limiting expected-risk expression gives $f^\star=1$
for every $a$. As $\norm{\Delta}\to0$ the formula approaches the same limit.
As $\sigma\to0$ we get $c\to0$ and
$f^\star=\min\{1,1/a\}$. In the history-heavy case $a\ge1$, this becomes
$f^\star n_1=n_2$: the optimum \emph{equalizes the effective information} of
the two domains. When $a<1$, scalar forgetting cannot upweight the
underrepresented history, so the constrained optimum remains $f=1$. Between
these limits $f^\star$ interpolates according to $c$.

The optimal factor is therefore not a function of drift magnitude alone. It is
set by the ratio of accumulated information to mapping mismatch, measured in
units of noise, and forgetting acts as information rebalancing rather than
drift tracking.  This restates
Proposition~\ref{prop:fstar1} constructively: equal information ($a=1$) forces
$f^\star=1$ regardless of $\Delta$, which is why mapping drift alone cannot
license discounting in expected risk. Section~\ref{sec:history-dominance}
compares this expected-risk formula with a distinct finite-sample conditional
Oracle; the latter may select on realization-specific noise and is not an
exact verification target.

\begin{proposition}[Scoped train-only non-identifiability]
\label{prop:train_only_nonidentifiability}
Let $\mathcal I_{\rm tr}$ contain the complete training samples, deterministic
split identities and every sufficient statistic computed from them.  Without
an assumption linking training and test covariances, no deterministic rule
$S(\mathcal I_{\rm tr})$ can always select the balanced-test-risk-optimal
fixed factor, even for two scalar noiseless linear domains with equal training
information and $\lambda=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use identical training data in two worlds, with domain regressors $w_1=0$ and
$w_2=1$ and equal training information.  Hence both worlds have the same
$\mathcal I_{\rm tr}$ and produce
$W(f)=1/(1+f)$.  Their balanced test risk is
\begin{equation}
  L(f)=\tfrac12\{\rho_1[W(f)-w_1]^2+
                         \rho_2[W(f)-w_2]^2\},
\end{equation}
where $\rho_k=\E_{\rm test}[x_k^2]$.  In World A, let
$(\rho_1,\rho_2)=(1,1)$; the risk-optimal prediction is $1/2$, so
$f_A^*=1$.  In World B, let $(\rho_1,\rho_2)=(1,4)$; the risk-optimal
prediction is $4/5$, so $f_B^*=1/4$.  A deterministic rule sees identical
training information in both worlds and must return the same factor, which
cannot equal both optima.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:train_only_nonidentifiability} is deliberately narrow.
It concerns target-domain covariance information absent from
$\mathcal I_{\rm tr}$ under unrestricted train--test covariate shift.  It does
not imply failure under an i.i.d. train--test assumption, does not rule out
other source-side structural assumptions, and does not preclude a disjoint
target-calibration set from identifying the relevant risk weights.  Unlike a
general worst-case theorem for data-agnostic model merging, it fixes the RLS
model, loss, two-domain sequence and information permission used here.

\subsection{Datasets and Evaluation}

We use ShanghaiTech Part A (SHA; 300/182 train/test, mean count 541) and
Part B (SHB; 400/316, mean count 123)~\citep{zhang2016shanghaitech},
JHU-Crowd++ (JHU; 2272/500 train/val, mean count 372)
~\citep{sindagi2019jhu}, and UCF-QNRF (QNRF; 1201/334, mean count
815)~\citep{idrees2018qnrf}.
Evaluation: relative MAE ($\relmae = \mathrm{MAE}/\mu_{\mathrm{test}}$)
computed per domain; final score is the average across all domains in
the sequence (\emph{balanced multi-domain average}).
We also report each dataset's raw-count MAE and RMSE, plus nBwT
(negative backward transfer; higher = less forgetting of old domains).

FDST contains 15,000 annotated video frames from 13 scenes
~\citep{fang2019lstn}.  To preserve an untouched evaluation source, protocol
C7-fdst-v3 uses only official \texttt{train\_data}: six path-selected scenes,
one 150-frame video each, split by contiguous blocks into 90/30/30 fit,
validation and test frames.  The official test partition remains untouched;
the six scenes are one external source, not six replications.  Natural-data
runs use the frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 dual head of Section~\ref{sec:method}
with $\lambda=100$.  SHA, SHB, JHU and QNRF are development data; only FDST
is previously unseen.  Frozen claim sets are checked by manifests, locks,
complete-matrix validators and independent recomputation, with full audit
records supplied separately.

\subsection{Controlled Synthetic Boundary Study}

We preregister a balanced i.i.d. linear model with four sequential domains,
ten independent seeds and forward/reverse paired orders.  A complete $2^4$
design independently switches label scale, domain reliability, linear mapping
change and covariance anisotropy. The primary loss is population balanced
normalized MSE; the reverse order is a sensitivity check and is averaged
within seed before the paired-seed bootstrap. The fixed-$f$ Oracle sweeps the
frozen scalar grid on population risk and is diagnostic only. A cell has
material opportunity within that family only when mean gain over $f=1$ is at
least $0.5\%$ and its 95\% interval excludes zero.

All 16 cells fall below that threshold.  The largest fixed-factor Oracle gain
is only $0.200\%$ in the reliability-only cell (95\% interval
$[0.142\%,0.267\%]$).  The Null opportunity is $0.034\%$, below its separate
$0.1\%$ implementation-check ceiling; target normalization makes the entire
label-scale-on curve exactly equal to its scale-off counterpart; and estimated
precision decreases monotonically with noise in all 20 reliability-only
order rows. Thus the generator-direction checks pass even though no material
opportunity appears within the scalar-factor family.

\subsection{History Dominance and the Information-Balance Prediction}
\label{sec:history-dominance}

Corollary~\ref{cor:information_balance} predicts that the optimum is governed
jointly by the information ratio $a=n_1/n_2$ and the noise-to-mismatch ratio
$c$, not by drift magnitude alone. We compare it with a finite-sample
conditional Oracle in the same two-domain geometry, sweeping
$a\in\{1,\dots,1000\}$ and
$\norm{\Delta}\in\{0,\dots,1.6\}$ with $d=8$, $n_2=64$, $\sigma=0.5$,
$\lambda=0$, 20 seeds per cell and a $0.005$-spaced factor grid. This study is
exploratory rather than preregistered. For each random training realization,
the reported Oracle factor minimizes true population risk and is selected
before aggregation across seeds. It therefore measures conditional opportunity,
not attainability, and is not the same estimand as the expected-risk optimum in
Corollary~\ref{cor:information_balance}.

The estimands are associated ($r=0.943$, mean absolute deviation $0.058$)
but not identical.  At $\norm{\Delta}=1.6$ the Oracle is within $0.009$ of
$1/a$ on average.  At zero mismatch the expected-risk optimum remains one,
whereas nearly flat realization-specific risk curves let the conditional

gate in $5/10$ cells (maximum $1.57\%$).  This is selection on estimation
noise, not structural mismatch.  The result is exploratory and inherits the
two-domain, isotropic, $\lambda=0$ assumptions; it locates opportunity but does
not make $\norm{\Delta}$ and $\sigma$ identifiable to a train-only selector.

\subsection{Exploratory Candidate-Family Attribution}
\label{sec:candidate-family}

The scalar family couples all historical weights to one exponent, enforces
monotonicity, and keeps the incoming domain maximal.  An exploratory diagnostic
therefore compares four strictly nested, mean-normalized Oracle families on
the same generator: scalar, monotone, free with current-domain maximum, and
fully free, containing 21, 1{,}790, 9{,}280 and 177{,}059 candidates.  Their
mean gains are respectively $.067\%$, $.085\%$, $.106\%$ and $.224\%$.  Thus
full relaxation multiplies mean opportunity by $3.3$, yet
the largest cell reaches only $0.408\%$ and $0/16$ cells clear the material
gate.  Within this nested path, $75.4\%$ of the scalar-to-free increment occurs
when the current-domain-maximum constraint is removed.  The free family is
also exactly order-invariant in this generator, whereas the scalar family's
forward--reverse opportunity gap averages $.113$ points.  These are
population-risk Oracle measurements, not an attainable selector or evidence
about natural DINOv2 features.

\subsection{Development Diagnostics: Opportunity--Selection Gap}

The five-split C4 reliability diagnostic provides positive JHU headroom
(mean Oracle gain $7.42\%$), but the train-selected gain averages $3.50\%$
and the frozen $4/5$ decision gate fails jointly with QNRF.  QNRF has only
$0.031\%$ mean Oracle opportunity, yet the proxy reweights on two splits.
These dependent splits are a reproducible case study, not independent
replications or evidence for a reliability mechanism.

Across 40 correlated crowd configurations, the isotropic fixed-factor
predictor has moderate Oracle association (Spearman $0.498$) but worse factor
MAE than constant $f=1$ ($0.245$ versus $0.225$).  Its selected factor worsens
matched test MSE by $2.876\%$ despite $2.579\%$ diagnostic Oracle opportunity;
downstream relative-MAE utility is also negative ($-1.836\%$).  The clearest
order failure, SHA$\to$JHU$\to$SHB, loses $28.413\%$ matched MSE, while
SHA$\to$SHB has zero Oracle opportunity yet reweights in all five splits.
Thus correlation and decision accuracy do not control factor strength or
regret.  Complete per-seed tables and artifacts remain in the audited
supplement.

The common-$f$ theory is not an optimality guarantee for the deployed causal
sequence $(f_2,\ldots,f_T)$: its accumulated state depends on the full history,
so an adjacent-domain score alone is insufficient.  The failed calibration
does not prove that every train-only estimator is impossible; it bounds the
two frozen natural-data rules.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ==============================================================

The theory assumes isotropic features and a common fixed factor; real DINOv2
features are anisotropic and the deployed learner chooses a causal factor
sequence.  Its propositions are therefore boundary statements, not calibrated
predictions for the natural pipeline.  The non-identifiability example is also
narrow: it allows unrestricted train--test covariance shift and does not cover
i.i.d. deployment, structural shift assumptions, randomized rules or a
disjoint target-calibration set.

Every opportunity claim is conditional on a frozen scalar grid.  A null result
does not exclude per-domain, directional, spectral or matrix-valued weighting.
The exploratory free-weight result bounds this family gap only for one
generator; it remains an unattainable population-risk Oracle and does not
generalize to natural features.  Conversely, selector-v2's positive synthetic
KNN utility does not establish natural-image generalization or contradict the
unrestricted non-identifiability example.

Natural-data evidence is limited.  SHA, SHB, JHU and QNRF are low-resolution
development cases with dependent splits and reused orders.  FDST is one unseen
source, not six independent replications, and has no scalar-grid opportunity.
The fixed DCT-5 smoothing, dual-head mixture and $\lambda=100$ lack a
preregistered sensitivity analysis.  SIFt and DOS share the synthetic head and
risk but not update cost, and neither comparison covers the dense natural
head or deep distillation budgets.

Finally, normalized MSE and downstream relative MAE can disagree.  We report
both, retain paired uncertainty and avoid interpreting small or
selection-adjusted gains as reliable without the frozen gates.

\section{Broader Impact}
\label{sec:broader-impact}
% ==============================================================

Crowd-counting systems carry surveillance, privacy, consent and governance
risks.  This work studies aggregate labels rather than identity tracking, but
deployment still requires licensing, proportionality, data protection and
separate distribution-shift and subgroup-harm tests.  Null results here do not
imply that a deployed system is safe.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ==============================================================

We asked whether $f=1$ should always be used in analytic continual visual
regression and, if not, whether train-only sufficient statistics can reliably
select a better scalar factor.  The answer is conditional on the candidate
family.  The controlled study and unseen FDST source show no material
scalar-grid opportunity, while held-out synthetic tasks and crowd development
cases contain positive diagnostic headroom.  Thus $f=1$ is not universally
optimal, but opportunity is sparse and distribution dependent.

Selection is the harder problem.  Both frozen natural-data rules miss or make
harmful decisions.  The held-out KNN rule has positive utility and lower harm,
but higher opportunity-subset regret than the analytic rule, so the composite
criterion is not met and neither selector dominates.  These results do not
validate an adaptive algorithm.

The theory explains one boundary: in the two-domain isotropic model,
$f^\star=\min\{1,(1+c)/(a+c)\}$ depends jointly on information imbalance and
the noise-to-mismatch ratio.  No mismatch gives $f^\star=1$; dominant
mismatch in a history-heavy stream gives $f^\star n_1=n_2$.  Forgetting
therefore rebalances information rather than directly tracking drift.  A
finite-sample Oracle can still select on nearly flat, realization-specific
noise, and unrestricted train--test covariance shift prevents any deterministic
train-only rule from always identifying the test optimum.

The contribution is consequently a scoped boundary analysis and falsification
protocol, not an algorithmic-superiority or universal-impossibility claim.
Directional families, independent natural sources and stronger structural or
calibration assumptions remain open directions; FDST must not be reused to
tune a new selector after this single-shot evaluation.
